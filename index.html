A

SECTION 01

LANGUAGE

What the speaker builds with words and structures
START identify useful language → END explain and reuse how it is built.
A1 · Language Inventory → What was said?
  • A1.1 Words & phrases — identify important lexical items.
  • A1.2 Chunks & sentences — capture language as usable units.
  • A1.3 Key / repeated expressions — notice what carries meaning or recurs.
A2 · Vocabulary & Lexical Patterns → How does the lexis work?
  • A2.1 Key vocabulary — meaning in this context.
  • A2.2 Collocations & word partnerships — words that naturally go together.
  • A2.3 Phrasal verbs, idioms & fixed expressions — spoken lexical patterns.
  • A2.4 Formulaic language, chunks & preposition patterns.
  • A2.5 Register — formal, neutral, informal or conversational choice.
A3 · Grammar → How is meaning grammatically built?
  • A3.1 Tense & aspect — time, completion and ongoingness.
  • A3.2 Modality — certainty, possibility, obligation and stance.
  • A3.3 Conditionals — relationships between situations and outcomes.
  • A3.4 Questions & negation — form, purpose and spoken use.
  • A3.5 Ellipsis, fragments & spoken grammar — what is omitted or simplified.
A4 · Syntax & Discourse → How is the message organised?
  • A4.1 Sentence & clause structure.
  • A4.2 Word order & information structure — what is foregrounded.
  • A4.3 Linking & reference — how ideas connect.
  • A4.4 Cohesion & topic development.
  • A4.5 Discourse markers — how the speaker moves the message forward.
YOU LEAVE WITH: a clear map of the language forms worth noticing and using.
B

SECTION 02

DELIVERY

How the language becomes actual speech
START hear the words → END reproduce the speaker's spoken delivery.
B1 · Pronunciation → How are sounds produced?
  • B1.1 Individual sounds — notice difficult or reduced sounds.
  • B1.2 Word pronunciation — identify the spoken shape of key words.
B2 · Stress & Rhythm → What stands out?
  • B2.1 Word stress.
  • B2.2 Sentence stress — prominent information.
  • B2.3 Rhythm — strong and weak beats across the phrase.
B3 · Intonation → How does pitch carry meaning?
  • B3.1 Intonation patterns.
  • B3.2 Focus and emphasis.
  • B3.3 Attitude or interaction signalled through pitch.
B4 · Chunking & Pausing → Where does speech divide?
  • B4.1 Thought groups.
  • B4.2 Pauses — planning, emphasis or boundary.
  • B4.3 Chunking — speak in meaningful units rather than isolated words.
B5 · Connected Speech → What changes in fluent speech?
  • B5.1 Linking.
  • B5.2 Assimilation.
  • B5.3 Elision.
  • B5.4 Weak forms & reductions.
  • B5.5 Combined connected-speech patterns.
B6 · Fluency → How does the speech flow?
  • B6.1 Speed and pacing.
  • B6.2 Hesitation and planning.
  • B6.3 Smoothness, chunking and continuity.
YOU LEAVE WITH: a spoken-performance model you can hear, imitate and reproduce.
C

SECTION 03

COMMUNICATION

Why the speaker communicates this way
START understand the message → END understand the speaker's communicative choices.
C1 · Meaning → What does it mean here?
  • C1.1 Literal meaning.
  • C1.2 Contextual meaning.
  • C1.3 Intended meaning.
  • C1.4 Implied meaning, ambiguity and meaning shifts.
C2 · Function → What is the language doing?
  • C2.1 Informing, explaining and describing.
  • C2.2 Examples, clarification and emphasis.
  • C2.3 Contrast, evaluation and persuasion.
  • C2.4 Hedging, agreement, disagreement and correction.
  • C2.5 Responding and managing the conversation.
C3 · Attitude & Stance → What does the speaker signal?
  • C3.1 Certainty / uncertainty.
  • C3.2 Confidence / doubt.
  • C3.3 Enthusiasm, caution and surprise.
  • C3.4 Approval, criticism and emotion.
C4 · Pragmatics → What is meant beyond the words?
  • C4.1 Direct vs indirect meaning.
  • C4.2 Implication and inference.
  • C4.3 Politeness, softening and intensification.
  • C4.4 Stance, face management and social meaning.
C5 · Context & Register → Why does this fit the situation?
  • C5.1 Speaker, listener, place and time.
  • C5.2 Relationship, topic and communication goal.
  • C5.3 Formal, neutral, informal and conversational style.
  • C5.4 Academic, professional, persuasive or speaker-specific style.
C6 · Interaction → How is the conversation managed?
  • C6.1 Taking, holding and giving the floor.
  • C6.2 Interrupting and overlapping.
  • C6.3 Responding and backchanneling.
  • C6.4 Clarification and repair.
  • C6.5 Follow-up questions, closing and transitions.
C7 · Choice & Alternatives → Why this form, not another?
  • C7.1 Why this word, phrase, grammar or tense?
  • C7.2 Why this discourse marker, pronunciation or level of directness?
  • C7.3 Synonyms and alternative phrases / grammar.
  • C7.4 More formal / informal or stronger / softer alternatives.
  • C7.5 Compare the meaning and effect of the alternatives.
C8 · Naturalness → Does this sound authentically spoken?
  • C8.1 Natural expression and common pattern.
  • C8.2 Context-dependent or spoken-only tendency.
  • C8.3 Written vs spoken difference.
  • C8.4 Native-like chunking and natural alternatives.
YOU LEAVE WITH: an explanation of meaning, function, attitude, context and communicative choice.

D3 · Speaking Transfer → Can I make it mine?

  • D3.4 Retelling and opinion.

  • D4.1 What did I understand?

  • D4.4 What can I use or say differently now?
  • D4.5 What can I transfer to real communication?

HOW TO USE THIS MAP

1. Scan the four sections first. 2. Open the section you need. 3. Follow its subsections and micro-focuses. 4. Do the detailed work inside that section. 5. Finish with the stated output and move forward.

THE CORE

From watching a video to understanding real spoken English.

This framework turns authentic speech into a layered learning object. Every useful piece of language can be examined through form, meaning, function, sound, context and communication — then reproduced and transferred into independent speaking.
